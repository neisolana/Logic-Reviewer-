Predicaments & Substance

Aristotle's 10 categories — the framework for all reality

What are Predicaments?

Predicaments are the fundamental categories into which all things can be divided. Aristotle outlined 10 categories as the basic ways of describing reality. They provide the framework for analyzing propositions, arguments, and the structure of thought.

⚗️ Substance

The essence or underlying reality of a thing. It exists in itself — a "being-in-itself." Not dependent on anything else.

man tree Socrates stone

✨ Accidents (9 types)

Properties that exist in another — a "being-in-a-substance." Cannot exist on their own; they depend on substance.

quality quantity relation + 6 more

The 9 Accidents — Deep Dive

Quantity makes things concrete and measurable. A zebra exists with height, width, and depth — we can see and measure it. Materiality brings quantity.

Quality is recognized before quantity in daily life. We identify a zebra's quantitative features only because we first recognize its colors and shape.

Something can be compared with something else. Relation can only be known through comparison — you can only know if someone is taller or shorter relative to another.

Judgment & Propositions

Categorical propositions, AEIO types, and structure

💡 Judgment

An act of the mind that affirms or denies something. It expresses a statement that can be true or false. Expressed through a proposition.

📄 Proposition

A declarative sentence that can be either true or false — never both. Must be declarative (not a question or command).

⭐ Exam Tip: "Open the door" = NOT a proposition (command ❌). "The Earth revolves around the Sun" = proposition ✓

Structure of a Proposition

PartRoleExample: "All nurses are compassionate."

  • All nurses are healthcare workers.

  • All humans are mortal.

E Examples

  • No dogs are cats.
  • No apples are vegetables.
  • No clouds are cotton candy.

I Examples

  • Some students are athletes.
  • Some dogs are friendly.
  • Some fruits are sweet.

O Examples

  • Some students are not athletes.
  • Some dogs are not friendly.
  • Some fruits are not sweet.
⭐ Key Distinction: "All" = EVERY member. "No" = ZERO members. "Some" = AT LEAST ONE or more.

Types of Definition

Nominal · Real · Essential · Accidental · Rules of Good Definition

📖 Nominal Definition

Explains the meaning of the name/term. Focuses on language, not the actual nature of the thing.

Hypertension = high BP Analgesic = pain reliever Dyspnea = difficulty breathing

🌿 Real Definition

Explains what the thing truly is. Gives its essential characteristics or nature. Goes deeper.

Man = a rational being Logic = science of right reasoning

Nominal vs. Real — Side by Side

NominalReal
Explains what the name meansExplains what the thing is
Focuses on languageFocuses on essence/nature
"Hypertension" = high blood pressure"Man" = rational being

⚙️ Essential Definition

Constructed by Genus + Specific Difference. Captures qualities shared by all members AND what distinguishes them.

  • "Triangle is a polygon (genus) with three sides (specific diff)."
  • "Man is an animal (genus) that is rational (specific diff)."

🎭 Accidental Definition

Uses characteristics NOT essential to the thing's nature — could change without changing what it fundamentally is.

  • Hair color of humans
  • "A car with speed of 130 kph"
  • The multicolored school uniform
✦ Genus + Specific Difference Formula
Genus = the larger class the thing belongs to
Specific Difference = what sets it apart from others in that class
"The Bible is a religious book (genus) sacred to Christianity (specific diff)."

3 Types by Method

Makes meaning clear by applying the term to real examples. Two subtypes:

  • Ostensive — showing a type of thing
  • Extensive — naming everything in the group

Examples: "Oxygen therapy → nasal cannula, face mask, oxygen tank" · "Blood types → A, B, AB, O"

Explains a term by describing observable characteristics or features — enumerates traits.

Example: "An animal that can bark, guard, and show loyalty." → dog

6 Rules of a Good Definition

#RuleViolation Example
1Not Too Broad"Bird is an animal." → dogs are animals too!
2Not Too Narrow"Bird is an animal that can fly." → penguins can't fly
3Avoid Vagueness/Obscurity"Pain is an experience that varies significantly."
4Must Not Be Circular"Nursing assessment is the process of assessing a patient."
5Should Not Be Negative (unless necessary)"A healthy patient is not a sick patient."
6Should Not Be Slanted/Biased"Nurses are just assistants who follow orders."
⭐ Quick ID: Too broad = includes extra things. Too narrow = leaves out valid things. Circular = uses the defined word inside its own definition.
